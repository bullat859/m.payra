01 Introduction & Fundamental Architecture

This extraordinarily detailed and comprehensive Privacy Policy describes with absolute precision how Dynamic Edge Gesture Control (officially registered under the Application ID: com.m.payra.gesture.control.app), meticulously developed and maintained by Madhusudan Payra ("we", "us", "our", or "the developer"), collects, utilizes, manages, processes, and ultimately safeguards your deeply personal and sensitive device information. In an era where digital privacy is constantly under siege by invasive data collection practices, we believe that providing an exhaustive breakdown of our operational architecture is not just a legal requirement, but a profound moral obligation to our user base.

The application is designed fundamentally as an advanced utility meant to provide system-wide customization, edge navigation sliders, intuitive notch menus, hovering floating panels, and situational shortcuts that adapt to your daily workflow. To achieve this seemingly magical level of seamless integration with the Android operating system, the app operates on a strict, unyielding local-first processing model. What this means in practical terms is that your gesture triggers (whether they are rapid swipes, prolonged holds, or multi-finger taps), the complex touch detection mathematical routines, your meticulously crafted custom shortcuts, and even your personal encrypted quick notes are all computed, evaluated, and retained directly on your device's physical hardware. We do not run, maintain, or rent any external user-profiling cloud servers for core gesture logic. We never harvest your data to build behavioral profiles ourselves, and we absolutely never transmit or sell your personal files or interaction history to third-party data brokers.

By engineering the application to function autonomously within the confines of your device's local memory and storage, we physically eliminate the vectors through which data leaks typically occur. The Android sandboxing architecture ensures that our application exists in its own secure silo, unable to broadcast your gesture inputs to the wider internet without explicit network calls—which we have strictly quarantined solely to our carefully curated advertising mediation layers. Our philosophy is simple: what happens on your screen, dictated by your fingers, is none of our business.

By continuing to install, configure, and utilize Dynamic Edge Gesture Control, you explicitly acknowledge, comprehend, and unconditionally agree to the highly specific permissions model, the targeted hardware usage requirements, and the transparent multi-SDK advertising integrations outlined in agonizing detail within the sections below.

02 Exhaustive Manifest Permissions Disclosure

To properly deliver system-wide, universally responsive gestures and highly advanced overlay automation without causing system instability, our application must request explicit, highly privileged permissions from the Android OS. Every single permission listed in our `AndroidManifest.xml` file is strictly scoped, continuously audited, and bound inherently to features you manually configure. Below is an exhaustive technical breakdown of what we ask for, and precisely why we ask for it:

🛠️ Accessibility Service API (BIND_ACCESSIBILITY_SERVICE)

Core Required Permission

The Accessibility Service API is undeniably the foundational engine and beating heart of Dynamic Edge Gesture Control. Without it, the application literally cannot function. While Android presents a frightening, highly generalized warning when enabling this service (claiming the app can "view your screen and observe your actions"), it is vital to understand the nuanced reality of how we implement it. It is strictly and exclusively utilized to:

  • Draw and maintain touch-sensitive gesture overlay handles: This allows us to render invisible or translucent hitboxes (such as edge sliders along the bezel, dynamic notches at the top of the display, floating islands, and expanding radial pie menus) securely on top of all currently active screens, bypassing standard window manager limitations.
  • Analyze coordinate-specific touch events: We must meticulously calculate and detect your swipe vectors, tap velocities, long-press durations, and drag distances specifically and exclusively within these pre-configured edge handle zones. If you touch outside our handles, the Accessibility Service ignores the input entirely.
  • Execute global Android system actions: Once a valid gesture vector is recognized, the service dispatches global intents assigned to your gestures. This is how we programmatically trigger system-level commands like Back, Home, Recent Apps, expanding the Quick Settings panel, entering Split Screen mode, and invoking the hardware Power Menu without requiring a rooted device.
Strict Accessibility Privacy Guarantee: We solemnly swear that we do NOT use the Accessibility Service API to read your screen content, scrape text from your messaging apps, inspect your keyboard keystrokes, intercept banking passwords, or log personal communications. Our Accessibility node configuration is explicitly tuned to ignore view hierarchies and focus solely on spatial touch coordinates within our designated edge zones.

⚙️ Foreground Services & Complex Lifecycle Management

Modern Android operating systems are incredibly aggressive when it comes to battery management and memory reclamation. To prevent the OS from arbitrarily killing the gesture engine while you are in the middle of a swipe, we must employ complex Foreground Services.

  • FOREGROUND_SERVICE & FOREGROUND_SERVICE_SPECIAL_USE: These declarations mandate the OS to keep our gesture recognition algorithms alive in high-priority background memory. By tying the service to a persistent, user-visible notification, the system understands that terminating this process would actively disrupt the user's navigational experience.
  • FOREGROUND_SERVICE_CAMERA: Introduced by stricter privacy rules in Android 14+, this specific foreground subtype is absolutely required when you trigger a flashlight toggle or a rapid camera gesture tool while our app is running as an active background service. Without this, the system throws a security exception.
  • FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MICROPHONE: Similarly required by Android 14+, this is invoked uniquely and exclusively when a user binds an edge gesture to capture instant, covert voice memos. It guarantees that the OS knows exactly when the microphone is engaged from the background.
  • FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PLAYBACK: This powers the offline media widget and the dynamic notch playback controls, allowing our overlays to intercept and display album art, track names, and playback state without halting when you lock the screen.
  • RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED: This allows the application to automatically re-initialize your active edge touch zones, floating panels, and background listeners the moment your device finishes booting up. Without this, you would have to manually open the app every time you restarted your phone.
  • WAKE_LOCK & REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS: We use partial wake locks to prevent the CPU's deep sleep routines from completely disconnecting the edge handle touch sensors. Furthermore, prompting the user to ignore battery optimizations is crucial for smooth, lag-free responsiveness during rapid sequential swipes.

🪟 System Overlays, Haptics & Advanced Device Controls

To truly customize the Android experience, our app must be capable of rendering graphics outside its own standard window constraints and interacting with fundamental hardware feedback mechanisms.

  • SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW: This is the critical "Draw over other apps" permission. It facilitates the rendering of floating gesture indicators, dynamic notch menus, and complex pie overlays perfectly on top of third-party applications, games, and the launcher. It is important to note that this permission strictly allows for UI rendering; it categorically cannot be used to inspect, record, or scrape the graphical elements of the applications situated beneath the overlay.
  • WRITE_SETTINGS: A powerful system-level permission that allows your swipe actions to dynamically write to the secure settings provider. This enables instant toggling of system brightness sliders, volume overrides, automatic rotation locks, and custom display timeout intervals directly from a swipe gesture.
  • MODIFY_AUDIO_SETTINGS & VIBRATE: These allow the application to programmatically modify audio stream levels (media, ring, alarm) directly without opening the volume panel, and generates highly tuned, tactile haptic feedback pulses upon the successful completion of an edge swipe.
  • BLUETOOTH_CONNECT & BLUETOOTH_SCAN: These relatively new, hyper-specific permissions are used to directly toggle your device's Bluetooth radio states, initiate scans, and rapidly connect or disconnect from paired wireless headsets or smart accessories straight from your customized quick gesture pie menus.

📍 Location Services (Foreground & Background Geofencing)

Required Declarations: ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION, ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION, and the highly sensitive ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION.

While a gesture control app requesting location might seem counterintuitive at first glance, it is employed exclusively and voluntarily for our advanced Situational Geofenced Triggers. For example, you may want to automatically disable all edge triggers when you arrive at a specific GPS coordinate (like your workplace) to prevent accidental swipes during meetings, or change the swipe actions when you arrive at the gym. We utilize the Android Geofencing API to set these spatial boundaries. Crucially, all geofence entry and exit events are evaluated entirely on your device's silicon. Your coordinates, movement history, and location profiles are absolutely never logged, transmitted to our servers, or shared with location brokers. It is a closed-loop system designed purely for local automation.

🔔 Notifications, Do Not Disturb Intercepts & Precision Alarms

To interface deeply with your device's alert states and scheduling capabilities, we require specific pathways to the notification manager and alarm manager.

  • POST_NOTIFICATIONS: A standard requirement in modern Android to post our mandatory foreground service notices (which keep the app alive) and to display rich, interactive media controls within the notification shade.
  • ACCESS_NOTIFICATION_POLICY: This grants the application the authority to read your current interruption filter state and enables you to map rapid gestures to effortlessly toggle complex Do Not Disturb (DND) modes, including specific priority-only configurations.
  • SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM & USE_EXACT_ALARM: Unlike inexact alarms which the OS batches to save battery, these permissions allow for the hyper-precision scheduling of automated profile switches, delayed gesture macros, or custom reminder timers that must execute at the exact millisecond triggered by your initial gesture shortcut.

📸 Camera, Microphone, Extensive Storage Access & Screen Capture

These permissions grant access to the most sensitive hardware on your device. We request them strictly to enable robust, on-the-fly media creation and management through simple swipes.

  • CAMERA: We hook into the Camera2 API on demand specifically to rapidly toggle the device's LED flashlight module and to capture immediate, silent photo or video records when you trigger a mapped "Spy Cam" or "Quick Snap" gesture. The camera stream is never kept open longer than the exact duration of the capture.
  • RECORD_AUDIO: This hardware pathway is activated only when executing a specific voice recording action assigned to an edge gesture. The raw audio buffer is immediately encoded and dumped to a local file.
  • READ_MEDIA_AUDIO, READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE, & WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE: These storage APIs are comprehensively utilized to scan and load local music metadata for the audio controller widget, import custom icon images from your gallery to use in pie menus, and to securely serialize, save, and export encrypted backup JSON configuration files of your elaborate gesture setups.
  • LAUNCH_CAPTURE_CONTENT_ACTIVITY_ALLOWED: This specific flag facilitates native Android screen capture intent invocations directly via configured gesture swipes, allowing you to take screenshots without pressing hardware buttons or relying entirely on the Accessibility Service's global action.
Inviolable Local Storage Privacy Guarantee: We guarantee unconditionally that all photographs, audio memos, video files, and screenshots captured through our shortcut triggers are systematically saved directly into your device's private sandboxed directories or your public media storage folders. Not a single byte of this media ever leaves your device's physical flash memory or is transmitted to our servers.

📞 Direct Dialing, Address Book Contacts & Biometric Security

To bridge the gap between gesture controls and human communication, we require permissions that interact with your phone's cellular capabilities and identity verification hardware.

  • CALL_PHONE & READ_CONTACTS: This combination allows you to assign instant, direct speed-dial actions to a specific edge swipe (e.g., swipe down on the right edge to immediately call your partner). The address book is accessed locally, strictly to let you populate and select phone numbers inside the app's configuration UI. The contact list is never uploaded, synced, or analyzed off-device.
  • USE_BIOMETRIC & USE_FINGERPRINT: We utilize the Android BiometricPrompt API to protect the app's core settings menu and your private, in-app encrypted notes from unauthorized local access. When you authenticate, the verification occurs entirely within Android's hardware-backed Trusted Execution Environment (TEE) security enclave. Raw biometric templates (your actual fingerprint data) are deeply encrypted by the silicon and are mathematically impossible for our app, or any app, to access or read.

🛡️ Internal Broadcast Receiver Security

com.m.payra.gesture.control.app.DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION: This is an application-level signature permission we explicitly define. It acts as a cryptographic shield, ensuring that internal Intent broadcast communications between the app's various background services and foreground UI components cannot be intercepted, spoofed, or manipulated by malicious third-party apps installed on the same device. It guarantees that a rogue app cannot mimic a gesture trigger by sending a fake broadcast to our service.

03 Device Hardware Features & Rigorous Compatibility Mapping

Beyond software permissions, the Android OS requires applications to declare specific physical hardware dependencies in its manifest via <uses-feature> elements. These explicit declarations specify to the Google Play Store the exact physical capabilities required to support our extensive array of mapped gesture actions. Here is an exhaustive breakdown of the hardware we interact with:

• Telephony Subsystem (android.hardware.telephony): This informs the OS that the device must have a cellular radio and baseband processor capable of making standard voice calls, enabling our speed-dial and emergency call gesture shortcuts to function properly.

• Camera Optics & Autofocus Mechanics (android.hardware.camera, camera.autofocus): This ensures the device has a physical image sensor and the mechanical actuators required for autofocus, fully supporting our ability to invoke flashlight controls via the flash LED and execute quick, sharply focused photo capture triggers.

• Audio Input Transducers (android.hardware.microphone): This dictates the necessity of a physical microphone array, enabling our quick-action voice recording and audio memo shortcuts.

• Bluetooth & Low Energy Radios (android.hardware.bluetooth, bluetooth_le): Required to programmatically toggle hardware wireless radios and seamlessly manage GATT connections to your low-energy accessories and audio peripherals.

• Geospatial Location Sensors (android.hardware.location, location.gps, location.network): Declares the need for physical GPS receiver chips and Wi-Fi/Cellular network triangulation capabilities, which act as the core engine powering our situational geofence profile triggers.

• Advanced Touch Emulation (android.hardware.faketouch): An important technical declaration that ensures our basic drag, swipe, and multi-touch gesture recognition functions remain fully operational and accurately mapped across a much wider variety of non-standard touch screens, styluses, and simulated pointer devices, rather than failing on devices lacking a traditional capacitive screen.

Crucial Compatibility Note: All hardware features are utilized dynamically and conditionally at runtime. We employ strict `required="false"` manifest flags where appropriate. If your specific device lacks certain physical hardware (such as attempting to run the app on a Wi-Fi-only tablet that lacks cellular calling capabilities), those specific actions are gracefully caught, disabled in the UI, and logged locally, while all other unrelated gestures remain entirely and fully operational.

04 Comprehensive Package Visibility (QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES Policy)

Dynamic Edge Gesture Control fundamentally relies on declaring the android.permission.QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES permission. In recent Android iterations (Android 11 and above), Google introduced strict package visibility filtering, meaning apps can no longer see other installed apps by default. In accordance with Google Play's stringent High-Risk Permission policy, we have successfully justified that this broad visibility is strictly, undeniably required for the core, intended functionality of the application to exist:

  • Universal App Launch Gestures & Intent Resolution: To allow you to map absolutely any installed application on your device to a swipe gesture, notch menu item, floating bubble dock, or radial pie menu, our app must be able to query the PackageManager for an exhaustive list of all launchable intents. Without this permission, we would be blind to your installed apps, making custom app-launching impossible.
  • Intelligent Per-App Gesture Blacklists / Exclusion Profiles: One of our most powerful features allows the app to dynamically detect when specific foreground apps (such as camera viewfinders, immersive full-screen video games, or sensitive drawing canvases) are brought into focus. By querying the package name of the active window, we can automatically and instantly suppress our edge touch triggers to avoid catastrophic accidental touches during critical moments. We cannot filter what we cannot see.
Absolute Package Visibility Privacy Commitment: The complete inventory of your installed applications, their package names, and their usage states is queried, processed, and evaluated strictly on your local device hardware using on-the-fly memory structures. We fundamentally do not catalog, serialize, transmit, upload, or monetize the list of applications installed on your device. Your app drawer is your private business, and our code treats it as highly volatile, localized data that is never written to an external network socket.

🌐 Meta Audience Network Integration

To maximize our inventory fill rates and ensure sustainable revenue, we have intricately woven the Meta Audience Network (formerly Facebook Audience Network) SDK into our bidding mediation layer. 🎮 Unity Ads Monetization Engine

Required Manifest Dependencies: Intensive hardware-accelerated video rendering pipelines and robust local caching mechanisms.

As a formidable pillar of our rewarded unlock economy, we have deployed the robust Unity Ads SDK. Renowned for its high-fidelity video interstitial and rewarded video caching architecture, this engine pre-loads dense video assets into your device's temporary memory buffers to ensure zero-latency ad playback when you choose to unlock premium features. Unity processes anonymized device telemetry—such as device make, battery state, and network bandwidth—to determine the optimal video bitrate and contextually relevant ad campaigns without ever piercing the veil of your personally identifiable information.

📊 Firebase Diagnostics & Network State

Required Manifest Permissions: INTERNET and ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE.

These fundamental networking permissions strictly power communication with Google Firebase Crashlytics. When our application encounters a fatal error, unhandled exception, or anomalous memory leak, Crashlytics automatically generates a highly obfuscated, anonymized stack trace (a raw text log of which line of code failed). This stack trace is transmitted so we can patch the bug in the next update. Furthermore, the network state permission is used solely to check if you have an active internet connection prior to attempting to load rewarded ads from AdMob, Meta, or Unity, preventing unnecessary CPU wake-ups and saving your battery.

07 Data Retention, Sandboxed Deletion, & Transit Security

Unbreakable Encryption in Transit: Although our application relies heavily on local processing, the minimal necessary communications it does make (specifically, sending crash logs to Firebase or requesting ads from our mediation networks like AdMob, Meta, and Unity) use the highest standard of secure TLS/HTTPS cryptographic protocols. Man-in-the-middle attacks attempting to intercept these analytics packets will merely yield useless, encrypted cipher text.

Absolute Data Retention & Guaranteed Deletion Mechanisms: Because we do not use cloud databases, the lifecycle of your data is entirely in your hands. Local app configurations, elaborate JSON gesture mappings, custom encrypted note files, and rapidly captured media stay physically on your device's persistent storage until you enact one of the following terminal actions:

  • Granular In-App Deletion: You manually delete individual gesture triggers, purge specific media items, or securely wipe note strings from inside the app's configuration UI.

When this happens, the Android Operating System automatically and permanently wipes the application's local sandbox storage directory, completely vaporizing all settings, logs, and preferences associated with it.
